2,200 square feet of it

The barn is where the children end up — old and young alike — and none of them want to leave.

A pool table, shuffleboard, air hockey, side-by-side basketball, a pinewood derby track, a putting green cut into the floor, giant Connect 4, and a wall of arcade cabinets on free play. It is heated, it is enormous, and there is a full sound system for whoever takes charge of the music.

It is the easiest room in the world to lose an afternoon in, at any age.

Four acres

There is a lot of ground here, and all of it is yours.

The pool and spa sit together in one fully gated area we call the Oasis, looking out over the vineyard, with mature oaks shading the gathering spots around them. It stays open year round and warms up on its own through the summer; in the spring it can be heated on request for a fee, with a few days notice. Two hours of spa heating are included every day.

Dinner happens in the corral under string lights, and the evening usually ends at the fire pit. There is disc golf, there are lawn games, and there is a greenhouse and a vineyard to walk.

The animals are the part most guests remember. Peacocks, ducks and hens, all of them used to visitors. In February through May a seasonal pond fills at the bottom of the property.

The house

A look around

The house

Arranged so that everyone fits, and everyone can find a corner.

Most houses that sleep twenty are simply large. This one is laid out so a group of that size can settle in without anyone feeling crowded. The bedrooms are spread across the house rather than stacked down one hallway. The kitchen is stocked for people who actually cook. There are five and a half bathrooms, so the mornings stay peaceful.

The main house is single story. All six bedrooms are on one floor, and there are no stairs at the entry.

Rooms 5 and 6 share a bathroom between them, with two sinks in it, which is the arrangement two families travelling together usually want. The seventh bedroom is the Industrial Mini Mansion, a tiny home on the property with its own queen bed, kitchenette, full bathroom, and a lounge with a fireplace and a big smart TV. Windows run down both sides of it. It sits a little apart from the house, which makes it the quietest place to sleep. There are about six steps up to the door.

The question everybody actually asks

What do we do all day if we never leave?

Whoever is awake first walks down to the farm store and comes back with eggs. Somebody cooks. We take anyone who wants to come out to the coop, and the children work out which hen laid which egg before running off across four acres.

The pool opens whenever the first person is ready for it. The barn opens whenever a child wants it, which is most of the afternoon. By the middle of the day the group has usually scattered across the property and found its own rhythm — some in the water, some in the barn, some doing nothing at all in the shade.

By evening it is the spa, the fire, and a walk through the greenhouse and the vineyard for anyone who wants one. Nobody has driven anywhere. That is the point.

On the property, not in town

The farm stand is a ninety-second walk from the back door.

Mini Barn Market sits at the back entrance of the ranch. Guests wander down in pajamas for eggs and sourdough before anyone else is up. There are seasonal vegetables, baked goods, pasture-raised meats and honey, depending on the week.

It is the highest-rated thing we run, and it is fifty steps from where you are sleeping.

The practical part

Internet across the whole property, and a house that runs itself.

Cox internet reaches the main house, the barn and the tiny home, so a group this size can all be online at once without anyone hunting for a signal. Five of the seven bedrooms have a smart TV, and the primary’s is tucked away inside the armoire. Two rooms are left without one on purpose — one of them has a wall of books and a desk instead — for anyone who would rather do without. The barn has a projector and a sound system of its own.

Every exterior door is on a smart lock, so there is no key to hand over or lose. The air conditioning is connected, and the irrigation, the outdoor lighting and the barn lighting are all automated. The Oasis pool and spa run from our phones — tell us when you would like them warm and they will be ready when you arrive.

The ranch runs on solar. There is no EV charger yet; that comes when we add the battery backup.

One less thing to pack

The little ones are looked after before you arrive.

Four pack-n-plays, high chairs, and a good supply of toys. The pool and spa are inside one fully gated area. The main house has no stairs. Groups often arrive here with five or six children under five, and the property is set up for exactly that.

The bathrooms and the laundry room are stocked too — shampoo, conditioner, hand and body soap, and laundry soap. They are ours, they are chemical-free, and they are what we use ourselves. Bring your own if there is something you are loyal to; plenty of people do. But they are there, and we hope you like them. Some of what we make — the body and face soaps among them — is for sale down at Mini Barn Market, and we are slowly adding more.

Your hosts

We live on the ranch. How much you see of us is up to you.

You will probably spot us coming and going from the bird garden — the animals need tending whether there are guests here or not. It also means the birds, the garden and the farm store come with someone glad to show you around.

How much of that you want is entirely your call. Some groups would rather have the place to themselves for the week, and we stay out of the way. Others want to walk the garden, see how the plants grow, put seeds in willing hands, or pick whatever is ripe. Just tell us when you arrive. This is a farm stay, and our job is to make it the one you came for.
