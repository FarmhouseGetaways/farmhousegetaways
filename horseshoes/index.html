The pit

One pit on each hill, and the stream bed in between.

The horseshoe pit at Mountain Retreat is a regulation pit that plays across our seasonal stream, from hill to hill. You pitch from one sand pit, over the stream bed, to the stake on the far side. Then everyone walks across, and pitches back.

It is a game for every age at once. Grandparents who have pitched shoes all their lives and grandchildren throwing their very first one can share the same pit, and the same scoreboard.

The pits sit on a natural hillside, so the ground is uneven and slopes down to the stream bed. Sturdy shoes make the walk between them easy, and the little ones are happiest with a grown-up beside them at the pit.

Keep score

The scoreboard, right on your phone.

One to four players or teams. Pick each player’s shoe color, tap as each frame is settled, and the board keeps the count. It remembers the game on this phone if you step away for lunch.

The basics

Where to stand
Pitch from behind the front edge of your own pit, across the stream bed, to the stake on the far side. The stakes are 40 feet apart, the regulation distance. Younger players, and anyone who would like a shorter throw, are welcome to step forward.
An inning
Each player throws both shoes, then the other player throws both. In doubles, partners stand at opposite ends and play against the person at their end.
Ringer: 3 points
The shoe circles the stake. Tap +3.
Close: 1 point
The shoe lands within one shoe-width of the stake, about six inches, or leans against it. Tap +1.
Every shoe counts
Count each of your shoes on its own. Two close shoes: tap +1 twice. A ringer and a close shoe: tap +3 and +1. Nothing cancels, so it is the same for one player or four.
Winning
First to the score you picked wins: 11, 21 or 40. Tied at the end? Play two more innings.

    How to score

    1. Ringer 3 points

      The shoe circles the stake, so a straightedge laid across its two open ends would clear the stake.

    2. Close 1 point

      A shoe within one shoe-width of the stake. Too close to call? That is what the measuring stick is for.

    3. Every shoe counts

      Count each of your shoes on its own: two close shoes make 2, a ringer and a close shoe make 4. Nothing cancels, so it works the same with one player or four.

    4. Winning

      First to 11 for a short game, 21 for a backyard game, or 40 for a pro game.

    One way to score, whatever the size of the game.

    The stream

    The seasonal stream runs right through the game.

    A seasonal stream runs through the property, and the horseshoe pit plays straight across it. Most of the year it is a bed of river stones. After a good rain it runs.

    Walk off the deck and across the Fairytale Bridge, and you are on the trails: single track through manzanita and scrub oak, granite boulders the size of cars, and boardwalk where the ground gets wet in spring.

    An afternoon at the pit

    First to 11, then the rematch to 21. Maybe a 40.

    1. After lunch

      Someone takes the shoes off their hooks, the sand gets a quick pass with the rake, and the first game goes to 11.

    2. The rematch

      Whoever lost asks for another. This one goes to 21, and the measuring stick comes out for the close ones. Who knows, maybe a game to 40 after that.

    3. Later on

      The shoes go back on their hooks and everyone drifts toward the fire pit, still talking about that last throw.

    After the last game

    Then the fire pit, and the stars.

    We are far enough from town that the stars come out in full nearly every night. Most groups end up at the fire pit or in the hot tub, staying out much later than they meant to.
